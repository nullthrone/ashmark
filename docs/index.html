01 · Overview

What it is

Ashmark keeps a chronicle of one character's decisions and lets them have consequences. Some quests set one side against another: Ashmark remembers the side you take, marks quests that contradict it and records it when you break your word.

What you do shapes an archetype. Alts on your account who share a surname form a house with one family history. Party members who also run Ashmark learn your archetype and origin through addon messages, with nothing shown in chat, and rumours of your choices travel on from party to party. Everything is written into one event log per character and told back as sentences in the client's language.

Ashmark is built for World of Warcraft Forever only. The TOC files allow only the forever and camelot game types and exclude standard and classic. If the addon loads anywhere else, a runtime check (WOW_PROJECT_ID == WOW_PROJECT_CAMELOT, or an interface number from 16000 to 19999) prints one line and no module runs. Retail and Classic Era are not supported.

What it never does

  • No combat logAshmark never registers COMBAT_LOG_EVENT or COMBAT_LOG_EVENT_UNFILTERED. Forever closes the combat log to addons.
  • No StaticPopupConfirmations and the note editor use Ashmark's own dialog, so Blizzard's shared popups stay untainted.
  • No librariesBlizzard APIs only: no LibStub, Ace3, HereBeDragons or ChatThrottleLib. Map pins and the addon-message throttle are Ashmark's own code.

02 · Modules

Features

A core and nine modules. Each module can be turned off in the settings, and a module whose client API is missing turns itself off and names what it needs. Each entry below says where you meet it in the game.

  • Chronicle

    In gameChronicle tab

    Quests turned in and abandoned, first visits to a zone, levels, deaths, new standings with the conflict factions and Legacy, alliances, broken words, turns from your house, group sessions, first meetings and closed chapters. The tab follows the quest log: chapters on the left, the chosen chapter's sentences on a parchment page on the right.

    Sentences are built at display time, so they follow the client language. A chapter closes at a campfire, or on its own at login or reload once it holds more than 1,000 entries or its first entry is older than 7 days.

  • Conflicts

    In gameA line under the quest dialog · Decisions tab

    Accepting or turning in a quest on one side of a conflict allies you with that side. Standing alone also decides it, once exactly one side's faction reaches the conflict's level (Honored in every pilot conflict); one standing decides every conflict of that faction in a zone at once, with one line in the chronicle. An alliance by quest names its conflict by the title of its first quest, so two conflicts between the same factions in one zone read apart. A quest that contradicts your side shows a red line under the dialog:

    Contradicts your alliance with {faction}.

    Turning such a quest in breaks your word: the chronicle and the Decisions tab record it and your alliance moves to the new side. The Decisions tab lists the conflicts by zone, each under the title of its first quest with both sides, the side taken and since when, how often it was broken, and your total of broken words. Ten pilot conflicts ship with v1. Outside Zephras Isle, the Skyborne starting zone, the first of them begin around level 30 in Desolace.

  • Profile

    In gameHouse tab · archetype in tooltips, the chronicle and rumours

    Three axes from −100 to 100, read from what you do. Every value halves over 30 days.

    • Fidelity, principled to opportunistic: +2 per turn-in for your side, +5 for every 7 days an alliance holds (once for the alliances one standing formed together), −25 per broken word (−15 once you are an opportunist), −2 per abandoned quest, −1 per 10 gold of loot beyond quest gold in a day (at most −5).
    • Habitat, civil to wild: +1 per 10 minutes outdoors and not resting, −1 per 10 minutes in a rested area, +6 per rest at a camp, −1 per turn-in in a capital.
    • Mercy, merciful to ruthless: +1 for each calendar day you play without a kill, −3 per calm neutral creature killed, −2 per critter.

    The axis furthest from zero, at 34 or more either way, names the archetype: Oathkeeper or Opportunist, Wanderer or Town-dweller, Gentle soul or Ruthless soul; otherwise Pragmatist. The origin is the zone where the character spent the most time in its first 20 hours played with Ashmark installed.

    Mercy is an approximation. Without the combat log, a kill counts when you target a neutral, non-player creature that is out of combat, combat starts with it as your target, and it dies without being tapped by someone else. The test mode measures the accuracy at launch; if it is too coarse, the axis is replaced after launch.

  • World map

    In gameWorld-map pins

    Pins for conflicts (open, allied, broken; the tooltip names the conflict by its first quest), for the places where you broke your word and for the places where your chapters closed. Zone maps show all three; continent maps show conflicts only. Only you see them. Pins are placed after combat, never during it.

    No pilot conflict has a map position yet, because the quest givers' coordinates are missing from the source data. Until they are added, the map shows broken words and chapters. Addons that replace the world map may not show the pins.

  • Encounters

    In gameUnit tooltip line · Encounters tab · chat lines

    Every party or raid you form, however it formed, becomes a session; the raid a battleground puts you in does not, so its strangers never become contacts. When it ends, each member becomes a contact with times met, last place and how it went: a boss kill, a wipe, a shared camp, a kick or plain travel.

    Met: 3× · last Desolace

    The line appears for people this character has met; a second line names the archetype of an Ashmark user. When people you know join your group, each of the first three gets a chat line and the rest share one. The tab lists contacts newest first in class colour, with your notes (up to 120 characters) and a "Familiar face" marker from three meetings on. Contacts belong to the account, counts to each character. Ashmark reads members' names outside instances only.

  • House

    In gameHouse tab · a hint under the quest dialog

    Characters on this account who share a surname form a house. The tab shows its members by level, this character's three axes and archetype, inherited feuds (sides your relatives took that this character has not, one row per conflict under the title of its first quest), the family chronicle, family memory (people a relative has met three times or more) and the account's Legacy level when the client exposes it.

    Your house, {house}, stands against {faction}.

    Taking such a quest is recorded as turning from your house. It is not a broken word. Without a readable surname, /ashmark house <name> picks a house.

  • Campfire

    In gameCentre-screen notice

    Sitting down at a camp and staying until Camp Benefits takes hold counts as a rest. A rest closes the open chapter once it holds at least 20 entries or began at least 30 minutes ago. With a party present, the group session counts as a shared camp in Encounters, and Ashmark users in the party exchange rumours.

    Chapter IV closes · Desolace

    The camp aura IDs come from Wowhead's Forever data and are unverified. The sleeping bag's aura ID is unknown, so v1 counts no bedroll rests; a later version adds the ID once the probe has found it.

  • Character knowledge

    In gameItem tooltips · links · comparison tooltips

    An item that has never been in this character's bags, bank or equipment shows only its name, binding, slot, item level, type and requirements, and the line "Your character has never held this." Items the server still hides stay as the game shows them, and held items show in full.

    Quest rewards in the quest dialog stay in full while the "Quest rewards in full" setting is on. The first scan after install records what the character already holds, with no log entry per item; the bank follows on the first visit.

  • Rumours

    In gameChat lines

    Your alliances, broken words and turns from your house become rumours that travel from party to party among Ashmark users. Deeds that tell the same thing make one rumour: siding with one faction in several conflicts of a zone starts one rumour, not one per conflict, until it fades. A rumour you hear for the first time prints one chat line.

    People say the Oathkeeper from Desolace has sided with {faction}.

    Each hop costs 30 percent of a rumour's strength. From the second hop the place is the continent; from the third the faction is gone. Strength halves every 3 days, and a rumour below 20 fades. A rumour names an archetype, the conflict's zone and a faction, never a character; but party members who hear one of your own rumours first know it is yours, because the game names the sender of every addon message. A copy keeps its id, content and age from hop to hop, and its hops show how far it is from the deed, so anyone who knows whose deed a rumour is can tell from your copy whether you grouped with that player in the past week, or how many groups lie between you. Entering a zone can repeat one rumour about it or its continent: each rumour at most once a day, at most five rumour lines an hour. The module runs only while Shared fiction is on.

  • Shared fiction

    In gameNo surface of its own · feeds the tooltip line and the chronicle

    When someone new joins your party, Ashmark sends your archetype, origin, ruleset, axis values and addon version to party members who also run Ashmark, and they send theirs. Nothing appears in chat. Each side sees the other's archetype in the Encounters tooltip line, and archetype and origin in the chronicle:

    You travelled with Thalen (Oathkeeper from Westfall) and Mira.
Coming in v2

Traces: graves, extinguished camps and notes built from templates, left where something happened. A companion app exchanges them between sessions while the game is closed. v1 reserves the store (AshmarkDB.traces) and documents its format; no v1 code reads or writes it.

03 · Install and use

Getting started

Install

  • CurseForge: Ashmark is not published there yet.
  • From a release: download the zip of a tagged release from the GitHub releases page and extract it into World of Warcraft\_forever_\Interface\AddOns\, so that Interface\AddOns\Ashmark\Ashmark_Forever.toc exists. Restart the game. No release is tagged yet.
  • From the source: until then, download the repository as a zip, extract it into the same AddOns folder and rename the extracted folder to Ashmark. The development files in it are not loaded.

Open the window

Type /ashmark or /amk, or click Ashmark in the Addon Compartment menu at the minimap. A left click opens or closes the window; a right click opens the settings. Escape closes the window, and its title bar moves it.

The four tabs

  • Chronicle: the chapter list and the page of the chosen chapter.
  • Decisions: conflicts under collapsible zone headers, in the layout of the reputation window; your count of broken words at the top.
  • Encounters: the people you have met, in the layout of the friends list, with a note button on each row.
  • House: house name and Legacy, your axes and archetype, members, inherited feuds, family chronicle and family memory.

04 · Data

Privacy and network

What leaves your client

Addon messages with the prefix Ashmark, on the PARTY channel only, in the form 1|TYPE|field|field…. In a raid, PARTY should reach only your own subgroup, at most four recipients; the probe checks this at launch, and if PARTY fails in raids, Ashmark is silent there. Ashmark never sends on RAID, GUILD, WHISPER or a chat channel, and drops messages that arrive on them.

TypeFieldsSent
HIyour GUID, archetype, origin zone ID, ruleset, addon versionwhen someone you have not greeted joins your party; at most once per 30 s
PRyour GUID, fidelity, habitat, mercyright after every HI
RUrandom 8-character id, template (ALLIANCE, BROKE, TURNED), hops, strength, age in hours, archetype, faction ID, map IDafter a greeting from an Ashmark user, or a camp rest with a party, when someone present has not heard the rumour; at most 3 per round, one round per 2 minutes

Names, contacts, notes, peers, chat text and free text are never sent. A rumour carries no GUID and no name, but the game names the sender of every addon message, so party members who hear your own rumour first know it is yours, and a passed-on copy, which keeps its id and content on every hop, shows how many groups lie between its sender and the deed. Every field must match a fixed pattern and a message is at most 255 bytes; the sender refuses anything else and the receiver drops it. Ashmark accepts HI and PR only when the GUID belongs to a member of your party and is not already bound to another sender, RU only from a sender whose HI or PR it has accepted, and at most 20 messages per sender per minute: 3 HI and 3 PR at once, then one of each per 30 seconds, and 10 RU at once, then one per 40 seconds. At most 6 new rumours per sender in 2 minutes are kept. Its own queue stays under Blizzard's throttle.

Ashmark only receives when "Share with other Ashmark users" is off, on Forever trial and veteran accounts, which cannot send addon messages, and for the rest of a session in which three sends in a row fail with an unexpected result.

What is stored

One account-wide SavedVariable, AshmarkDB, in _forever_\WTF\Account\<ACCOUNT>\SavedVariables\Ashmark.lua. Characters are keyed by GUID; names are for display only. It holds:

  • chars: per character, its name, surname, class, race, sex, faction, ruleset and level; the event log; chapters, first zone visits, alliances, profile, IDs of items held, group sessions (at most 200), a chosen house name and counters. The log is compacted at login and once it passes 4,000 entries: ordinary entries older than 180 days by the realm's clock go, then the oldest ordinary entries until 3,000 remain, those the chronicle never shows first. Chapters, levels, alliances, broken words and house turns are kept; first meetings and sightings of Ashmark users are kept up to 2,000 together, sightings going first.
  • contacts: the people you grouped with, with your notes. Past 2,000, the oldest go, except those with a note or three meetings.
  • peers: up to 500 Ashmark users you met, with their archetype, origin, ruleset, addon version and profile.
  • rumours: up to 200 rumours, your own included and kept before anyone else's, and which peers have heard each.
  • settings, traces (empty, reserved for v2) and dev (the last 10 test reports, 20 probe records and 20 blocked-action reports).

Reset

/ashmark reset deletes this character's record (log, chapters, alliances, profile, held items and sessions) and starts a new one. Contacts, peers and rumours belong to the account and stay, but they lose this character's share: its meeting counts in contacts and its sightings in peers. A contact that no other character met and that has no note goes. /ashmark reset <name> does the same for another character of the account that you deleted or moved, so it stops counting in its house; it never names the character you are playing. /ashmark reset others deletes contacts with their notes, peers and every rumour this account did not start, and forgets whom your own rumours were told to. In every character's record it also removes the first meetings and sightings from the log, the stored group sessions with their members, and other characters' party senders; old group sentences then count companions instead of naming them. All three ask first in Ashmark's own dialog. To remove everything, delete Ashmark.lua and Ashmark.lua.bak from the SavedVariables folder while the game is closed.

Saved data from a newer Ashmark is only read, never written. Data in a shape Ashmark does not know is kept untouched in AshmarkDB.recovered. When a new record starts, Ashmark says so. If the account used Ashmark before, its saved data did not load: do not reload or log out, quit the game and replace Ashmark.lua with Ashmark.lua.bak before the next login.

05 · Locales

Languages

Ten client locales, the set the Forever client offers. There is no Italian, because Forever has none.

  • enUSEnglish, also enGB
  • deDEGerman
  • frFRFrench
  • esESSpanish (Spain)
  • esMXSpanish (Mexico)
  • ptBRPortuguese (Brazil)
  • ruRURussian
  • koKRKorean
  • zhTWTraditional Chinese
  • zhCNSimplified Chinese, China region

Only Ashmark's own sentences are translated. Faction, zone, quest, item, class and race names come from the client at runtime, in the client's language. German, French, Spanish, Portuguese and Russian sentences take the feminine form for a female character where the language needs it. A missing key falls back to English, and the test mode lists the keys the active locale lacks.

06 · Launch

Testing at launch

Forever launches on 4 November 2026 at 23:00 UTC. Until then Ashmark has run only against the mock client of its test suite. The test mode and the probe settle the open facts below in the live client.

Run

  1. Install the build and log in a Forever character. Turn on Lua error display with /console scriptErrors 1.
  2. Talk to a quest giver and open a quest's details page. Stay out of combat.
  3. Type /ashmark test. It runs every check, prints the failures and a summary, opens the Chronicle next to the quest dialog, shows a sample line under the dialog and takes a screenshot into _forever_\Screenshots. For the next 10 seconds it records whether the screenshot succeeded and any blocked action.
  4. Type /reload. This writes the results to SavedVariables; addon code cannot reload the interface itself. On a first install the test prints the new-record warning in place of the reload hint; if Ashmark never ran on the account, reloading is safe.
  5. Type /ashmark test again. The second run must find the first run's record, which proves the SavedVariables round trip.
  6. Type /ashmark probe in the open world, in a party, inside a dungeon, in a raid and at a camp, with a /reload after each. In the same session, accept a quest and start a fight: the probe records both. In a group it sends one HI and records its echo.
  7. For the Mercy check, kill 3 critters and 3 calm neutral beasts, then run /ashmark test. For Shared fiction and Rumours, group with another Ashmark user and side with a faction in a conflict, then run it again.

Results and what to send

Results are stored in AshmarkDB.dev: tests (last 10), probes (last 20) and blocked (last 20). Open an issue on GitHub and attach WTF\Account\<ACCOUNT>\SavedVariables\Ashmark.lua, the screenshots, the client language, the Experience Preset (Classic or Enhanced) and the text of any Lua error. The file also holds your characters, the people you grouped with and your notes on them; if you prefer, send only the dev table.

07 · Dataset

Contributing conflicts

A good conflict is one where the quest texts themselves set one side against the other. The sources are CSV files in data/conflicts/, one per area, UTF-8, one row per quest. The conflict columns repeat on every row of a conflict and must agree.

ColumnMeaning
conflict_idsnake_case, unique across all files
zoneuiMapID of the zone the conflict belongs to
themefeud, law, trade, nature, faith or other
honoredreaction (5 Friendly, 6 Honored, 7 Revered, 8 Exalted) at which standing alone forms the alliance; empty when no side has a faction
anchor_m, anchor_x, anchor_yoptional map pin: uiMapID and coordinates 0 to 1; all three or none
sideA or B
factionreputation faction ID of the side; empty for a side without one
questquest ID; a quest belongs to at most one conflict
quest_nameEnglish name, for review only; the addon reads the localized name from the client
verifiedtrue only after the quest was checked in the Forever client
sourcethe https page the quest was taken from
 
python3 tools/conflicts.py           # validate, write Data/Conflicts.lua, register new ids in registry.csv
python3 tools/conflicts.py --check   # validate; fail if Data/Conflicts.lua or registry.csv is stale (CI)

The validator refuses a header that differs from these columns, a value outside its rule, a source that is not https, a missing quest_name, a quest listed twice, rows of one conflict that disagree, a side with two factions or without quests, two sides with the same faction, honored without a faction and partial anchors. Commit the CSVs, registry.csv and the generated Data/Conflicts.lua together, and never edit the generated file by hand.

Conflict ids and sides are permanent once generated, because players' alliances are stored under them. registry.csv lists every id ever generated with each side's faction and quests, and the generator refuses data that drops an id, gives a side another faction or moves a quest to the other side. Rename or retire an id only with a row in renamed.csv (old_id,new_id,reason); stored alliances follow a rename, and a retired id is never reused.

Every row stays verified = false until it is checked in the client. The Conflicts test check reads every quest title and faction name from the client, so a run of /ashmark test shows which IDs resolve.

Provenance: the pilot set was researched on 30 and 31 October 2026 from Wowhead's Forever database and re-checked quest by quest. It holds 10 conflicts: Gelkis against Magram in Desolace and Shen'dralas (six), Booty Bay against the Bloodsail Buccaneers in Stranglethorn Vale (two) and the Windshapers against the High Order on Zephras Isle (two). The classic starter zones yielded no quest pairs whose texts oppose each other, so outside Zephras Isle the first conflicts begin around level 30; conflicts below that level are the most wanted contribution. Format details: data/conflicts/README.md.

Architecture

One addon: a core and nine modules in one private namespace. The core keeps one event log per character in account-wide SavedVariables; modules write facts into it and derive everything else from it. Units talk through a synchronous bus and a small set of public service functions. Every uncertain API is feature-detected, every value that can be secret is read through one guard, and a module that lacks a capability turns itself off and says so. Errors are caught per module; after 10 in one session that module stops. The contract every unit builds against is docs/ARCHITECTURE.md; the decisions behind it are in docs/DECISIONS.md and the platform facts in docs/research/forever-platform.md.

Checks

luacheck .                            # lint, Lua 5.1, configured in .luacheckrc
busted                                # specs under Lua 5.1, configured in .busted
python3 tools/conflicts.py --check    # conflict data is current
python3 -B -m unittest discover -s tools -p "test_*.py"   # the generator's own specs
python3 tools/toc_check.py --strict   # both TOCs agree, and every listed file exists

CI (.github/workflows/ci.yml) runs these five on every push and on every pull request, on Ubuntu 24.04 with Lua 5.1, luacheck and busted from LuaRocks.

Ownership

Every file belongs to exactly one unit, listed in section 9 of the architecture. A unit that needs a change in a file it does not own asks for it instead of making it, and adds files only as its own specs and mocks. Both TOCs list every file from the start, so no unit edits them.

Release

Add the release notes to the top of CHANGELOG.md, then push a tag that starts with v. The release workflow runs the BigWigs packager: it takes the notes from CHANGELOG.md, packages the Ashmark folder as described in .pkgmeta, leaves out the development files, replaces @project-version@ with the tag and creates the zip and a GitHub release. The upload to CurseForge needs the repository secret CF_API_KEY and the project ID, which both TOCs carry as ## X-Curse-Project-ID: 1777037. The workflow also passes WAGO_API_TOKEN for Wago. ## Interface: 16001 is the beta and test value; the probe reads the live value at launch.
